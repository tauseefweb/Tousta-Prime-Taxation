Tax insights

Short, plain-language explainers on common U.S. and Canadian tax questions.

Deadlines

An extension gives more time to file, not more time to pay

A U.S. extension (Form 4868) moves the filing date for most individuals to October 15, but tax owed is still due in April. Paying an estimate with the extension helps limit interest and late-payment penalties.

Cross-border

FBAR and Form 8938 are two different reports

The FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) is filed separately from your return when foreign accounts together exceed $10,000 at any time in the year. Form 8938 is filed with your return and has higher thresholds that depend on filing status and where you live. You may need both.

Cross-border

U.S. citizens in Canada still file in both countries

U.S. citizens and green card holders are generally taxed on worldwide income wherever they live. The foreign tax credit and similar relief often reduce or remove the U.S. tax, but the return and any foreign account reports are still required.

Individuals

Who needs an ITIN

An ITIN is for people who must file a U.S. return or be listed on one but cannot get a Social Security number. Applications use Form W-7 with identity and foreign-status documents, and an expired ITIN must be renewed.

Canada

Self-employed in Canada: file by June 15, pay by April 30

Self-employed individuals and their spouses generally have until June 15 to file a T1, but any balance owing is still due April 30, and interest starts after that date.

Canada

Benefits you may be missing

Many families miss credits such as the GST/HST credit or the Canada Child Benefit because they never filed a return or applied. Filing every year, even with no income, keeps your eligibility under review.
